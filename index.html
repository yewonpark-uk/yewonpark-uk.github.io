01 Digitising a local taxi service

From paper bookings to online airport pick-ups

Kakao Mobility · South Korea’s leading mobility platform

Product Manager · Guam launch · Sep–Dec 2021

A local taxi operator had managed reservations and dispatch by hand for over 20 years. An online booking had to give staff and drivers enough information to fulfil the trip.

  • My role. From South Korea, I worked with the operator and a technical team of more than 30 people to turn passenger, driver and dispatch needs into launch requirements.
  • What changed. Travellers could reserve airport pick-ups before arriving in Guam. Online reservations fed into the operator’s booking and dispatch tools.

02 First international launch

Launching car sharing in Kuala Lumpur

SOCAR · South Korea’s No. 1 car-sharing platform

Product Manager · Jul 2017–Oct 2018 · Based in South Korea

SOCAR’s first overseas launch needed a way for customers to book and access cars, and for the local team to run the fleet and support customers.

  • My role. I coordinated product work mainly from South Korea, with two visits to Malaysia. I worked with the Kuala Lumpur team on identity checks, payments, bookings and vehicle access, alongside fleet and customer support tools.
  • What launched. The first phase covered 240 vehicles across more than 100 pick-up locations. I also coordinated phased releases for vehicle delivery and one-way airport trips.

03 Public transport · More than 3 million users

Updating a live public transport app after seven years

Kakao Mobility · South Korea’s leading mobility platform

Product Manager · KakaoBus · Nov 2024–Mar 2025 launch

As map apps brought more transport options together, KakaoBus needed to expand beyond buses. Its users still depended on fast access to live bus information.

  • My role. I used surveys, usage data and market research to agree release priorities with engineering, design, marketing and strategy. We kept the most-used bus features and retired some features that were costly to maintain.
  • What changed. The update kept live bus information familiar and added metro journey planning. I also led the product work for the server migration and set up processes for ongoing maintenance.

04 Shared office operations

Connecting the member app with branch operations

Member app and internal management system

FASTFIVE · South Korea’s No. 1 shared office provider, similar to WeWork

Product Manager and UX Team Lead: Nov 2018–Jul 2021

Across more than 30 locations and around 10,000 members, branch and head office teams relied on spreadsheets for day-to-day operations. I mapped how information moved between members, branch teams and head office before leading UX planning for both systems.

  • Member app. Members could see information for their branch and make bookings for that location.
  • Internal tools. Teams could manage contracts, access permissions, bookings, notices and enquiries in one system.

01 / Independent product · 2026

Globee · Finding family activities close to home

I designed and launched a UK directory for families looking for classes, days out and holiday clubs. I used AI tools to help build the site and collect listings, and checked dates, prices and source links before publishing.

Visit the live Globee site ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Product experiment · Prototype

The decision. Area filters can hide nearby activities across a regional boundary. I prototyped a search that puts the closest activities first and keeps location and age filters when switching between Classes and Days out.

What I tested. Using a simulated location, I checked the sort order, filter behaviour and how saved activities compared on a narrow screen.

What the audit found. Many listings lacked venue coordinates. A reliable distance sort depends on checking those locations first. The prototype is still separate from the live site.

05 / How I work

Find clarity.
Move together.

  1. Understand the whole service.

    Start with the realities of the people who deliver it.

  2. Make uncertainty visible.

    Within the time available, distinguish what is known from what is still uncertain.

  3. Agree what matters most.

    Set priorities with the business and the team.

  4. Deliver together.

    Align the team around the target date and work towards it together.
